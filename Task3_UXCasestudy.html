Abstract

This case study conducts a structured UX evaluation of Zomato, India's leading food delivery platform serving over 12 million active users across 800+ cities. The analysis applies Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics, user journey mapping, and competitive benchmarking to surface experience gaps.

Through systematic audit, three high-impact improvement opportunities are identified: restructuring the search and discovery architecture, reducing order-flow cognitive load, and redesigning the post-delivery feedback loop. Each improvement is accompanied by annotated mockup sketches and implementation rationale.

Method: Expert heuristic review + secondary user research synthesis · Tools: Figma, cognitive walkthrough · Scope: Mobile app (iOS/Android)

Analysis

Strengths & Weaknesses

✅
Strengths
  • +
    Live GPS tracking is smooth, reliable, and visually satisfying — one of the best delivery tracking UIs in the market.
  • +
    Review and rating system is rich — photo uploads, helpfulness votes, and verified purchase badges build trust.
  • +
    Zomato Pro membership savings are clearly communicated pre-checkout — drives real conversion uplift.
  • +
    Food photos are consistently high quality, well-curated, and enticing — reduces pre-purchase anxiety.
  • +
    Payment options are exhaustive — UPI, cards, wallets, BNPL — reducing drop-offs at checkout.
⚠️
Weaknesses
  • −
    Homepage is an attention war — too many content types (food, dining, grocery, live events) compete without clear hierarchy.
  • −
    Checkout flow has 5+ steps with multiple upsell modals, creating friction and drop-offs at peak order intent.
  • −
    Post-delivery rating prompt is abrupt, mandatory-feeling, and offers no incentive — resulting in low quality feedback.
  • −
    Search filters reset between sessions, forcing repeat setup — particularly painful for users with dietary preferences.
  • −
    Restaurant estimated delivery times are often optimistic, eroding trust when orders run significantly late.
05
Proposed Redesign

3 Improvement Proposals

01
Discovery & Navigation
Intent-First Home Screen Architecture
Redesign the homepage to open with a "What are you in the mood for?" intent selector rather than a cluttered promotional grid. Surface personalised context (time of day, past orders, location) before banners. Banners become a secondary carousel below the fold.
Current Problem
Users land on a homepage with 6+ competing banners, a Zomato Live tab, Grocery shortcut, Dining Out card, and algorithm-driven restaurant feed — all above the fold. There is no clear visual hierarchy. Users with a clear intent (e.g. "I want biryani") must scroll past irrelevant noise to reach the search bar or relevant category. This contributes to decision fatigue and reduces time-to-order.
Proposed Solution
Introduce a context-aware intent bar at the very top: a single large input combining location + search intent with smart suggestions. Below it, show 4 large mode tiles: "Order Now", "Schedule", "Dining Out", "Grocery" — clean, icon-forward, no text clutter. Personalised restaurant cards follow. Promotional banners demoted to a swipeable row below the restaurant cards. Filter persistence enabled via profile-linked preferences.

02
Checkout Flow
Streamlined One-Screen Checkout
Collapse the 5-step checkout into a single scrollable sheet with progressive disclosure. Remove intrusive upsell modals during the payment step — relocate upsells to a non-blocking "You might also like" strip within the cart itself.
Current Problem
The current checkout requires navigating: Cart → Address confirmation → Coupon screen → Upsell modal → Tip screen → Payment selection → Payment → OTP → Order confirmation. Each screen transition adds cognitive load. The upsell modal appears at the moment of highest intent, causing interruption anxiety. Users on slower devices or connections drop off during loading transitions between these steps. A/B studies in e-commerce consistently show that reducing checkout steps increases conversion by 15–30%.
Proposed Solution
Single bottom-sheet checkout that expands sections inline: (1) Delivery address with map pin preview, (2) Cart summary with inline quantity edit, (3) "Add more from this restaurant" expandable strip, (4) Coupon field, (5) Payment method quick-select with Apple Pay / UPI front-and-centre, (6) Place Order CTA with clear total. Tip moved to a post-delivery prompt. Eliminates 3 full-screen transitions and 2 modal interruptions. Retains all functionality without the friction.

03
Feedback System
Gamified & Contextual Review Loop
Replace the abrupt post-delivery rating prompt with a delayed, gamified, context-aware feedback system that feels rewarding rather than obligatory. Tie ratings to Zomato coins (loyalty currency) and surface prompts 30 minutes post-delivery — when the meal experience is fully formed.
Current Problem
Zomato's current rating prompt fires immediately after the delivery confirmation — before the user has even tasted the food. The 5-star tap plus mandatory text box feel like a chore. There is no incentive to rate, so most users skip it or leave generic 5-stars without useful feedback. This degrades the quality of social proof that drives restaurant selection for other users. The review system — one of Zomato's core differentiators — is undermined by poor feedback UX.
Proposed Solution
Introduce "Taste Check-In" — a delayed push notification 25–35 minutes post-delivery: "How was your [dish name] from [restaurant]?" Single tap opens a micro-review card: animated emoji sentiment selector (horrible → amazing), photo upload with one-tap from camera roll, optional text — not mandatory. Completion earns "Zomato Coins" displayed as an animated reward. A "Top Reviewer" badge unlocks after 10 quality reviews — drives social identity and long-term retention. Review quality measured and improved through ML sentiment analysis on submissions.

Summary

Conclusion

"Zomato succeeds where it matters most — trust, tracking, and content depth — but falls short in reducing friction at the moments of highest purchase intent."
Zomato is a technically robust, content-rich platform that has built genuine user trust through its live tracking system, extensive review ecosystem, and broad restaurant network. However, its UX suffers from accumulated product complexity — each feature addition over the years has left the interface slightly more cluttered, slightly more interruptive, and slightly more exhausting for users who simply want to eat well without friction. The three improvements proposed in this case study — intent-first discovery, streamlined checkout, and gamified reviews — are not radical overhauls. They are focused, high-leverage interventions that remove friction at the three most pain-sensitive touchpoints in Zomato's user journey. Each is rooted in established UX principles (progressive disclosure, recognition over recall, intrinsic motivation) and validated by analogous improvements in comparable platforms like DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Swiggy. If implemented, these changes would not only improve measurable metrics (conversion rate, review volume, session depth) but would make Zomato feel less like a feature catalogue and more like a trusted, frictionless food companion. That shift — from capable to delightful — is where durable user loyalty is built. Ultimately, Zomato's UX opportunity is not about adding more. It is about doing less, better — and trusting users to find what they came for.
