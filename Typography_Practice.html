02 — Visual Hierarchy
The Silent Voice
On the invisible power of type

Hierarchy in typography is not about making things look important — it is about guiding the reader's eye through a deliberate path. Size creates entrance. Weight builds emphasis. Space gives breath. Colour signals meaning. Together, they form a silent grammar that operates beneath the threshold of conscious thought.

Every font choice is a decision about tone. Every size adjustment shifts the balance of attention. The reader never notices good hierarchy — they simply follow it, effortlessly, as if there were no design at all.

05 — Psychological Effect of Type
Trust

Serif fonts with consistent stroke contrast communicate reliability and institutional authority. Banks, legal firms, and newspapers have long relied on this psychological effect to anchor credibility.

Libre Baskerville · 700 Bold · −0.01em tracking
ENERGY

Bold geometric sans-serifs with wide tracking and uppercase treatment signal momentum and urgency. Sports brands, tech startups, and call-to-action typography use this to create forward motion.

Outfit · 700 Bold · +0.06em tracking · uppercase
Calm

Italic serifs at regular weight feel thoughtful, unhurried, and literary. They slow the reader down deliberately — a whisper rather than a shout. Used in wellness, poetry, and editorial contexts.

Playfair Display · 400 Italic · +0.01em tracking
Luxury

Spaced capitals in a refined serif with restrained gold tones create distance and exclusivity. Fashion houses and premium brands use generous letterspacing to imply scarcity and deliberateness.

06 — Alignment Modes
Left
The eye enters at the left margin and finds comfort in consistency. The most natural choice for extended reading in Latin scripts.
Center
Formal, ceremonial, and balanced. Creates symmetry and calm authority. Best reserved for short headlines and invitations.
Right
Unusual and deliberate. Creates tension and draws the eye. Used in editorial captions, pull quotes, and art-directed layouts to break expectation.
Justified
Clean and architectural — both edges locked to the margin. Requires careful word spacing to avoid rivers of white. A long tradition in newspapers and books.
